Natural Pest Control at Home: Safe Ways to Keep Bugs Out Without Chemicals

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-11-09 · Category: Eco-Friendly Home

Finding ants in the kitchen or moths in the wardrobe is annoying, but reaching for a chemical spray is not the only answer. Most household pests are looking for the same three things: food, water, and shelter. Take those away, block their way in, and use a few natural repellents, and most pest problems solve themselves.

Start by Blocking the Way In

Pests do not appear from nowhere; they walk, crawl, or fly in through gaps you probably never notice. Check the seals around doors and windows, and add draught excluders or weather strips where you can see daylight. Seal cracks around pipes, skirting boards, and vents with caulk or fine mesh. A door sweep on an outside door closes the most common entry point of all. This is the same sealing work that saves energy in winter — our guide to draught-proofing your home covers the same gaps, so one job does two things at once.

Keep Food and Water Out of Reach

Most indoor pests are really a food storage problem. Store dry goods such as flour, rice, sugar, and cereals in sealed glass or hard plastic containers — pantry moths chew through paper bags and thin plastic with ease. Empty kitchen bins regularly, and keep the bin area clean and dry. Fix dripping taps and wipe up standing water under sinks, because cockroaches and silverfish are drawn to damp spots. A dry, tidy kitchen with sealed food is simply not an attractive home for pests, and this alone prevents most infestations before they start.

Natural Repellents That Actually Work

Many strong-smelling natural substances repel insects, and they are safe to use around the home. The trick is to use them consistently and in the right places, not just once.

Ants

Ants follow scent trails, so the first step is to wipe trails away with a mix of equal parts water and white vinegar. A line of cinnamon, coffee grounds, or chalk across a doorway can block their path, and a few drops of peppermint oil on a cotton ball near entry points keeps them away.

Fruit flies

Fruit flies breed in damp, sugary places. A simple trap works well: a small jar with a little apple cider vinegar and a drop of washing-up liquid, covered with cling film with a few tiny holes poked in it. Flies crawl in and cannot get out.

Clothes moths

Moths eat natural fibres, especially wool and silk. Store out-of-season clothes in sealed bags or boxes, and wash or air clothes before putting them away — moths are attracted to body oils and food stains left on fabric. Cedar blocks and dried lavender sachets in drawers and wardrobes help repel them.

Spiders

Most house spiders are harmless and eat other pests, but if you prefer them outside, a spray of water with a few drops of peppermint or citrus oil around window frames and corners encourages them to move on.

Safe Traps for Stubborn Pests

When repellents are not enough, simple traps do the job without chemicals. Sticky traps placed along skirting boards catch silverfish and cockroaches and also show you where they are coming from. Humane catch-and-release traps work for mice, but place them along walls where mice travel, and check them daily. For pantry moths, pheromone traps catch the males and break the breeding cycle. If you have pets or small children, choose enclosed traps and keep them out of reach — the same safety-first thinking as our guide to green cleaning that is safe for kids and pets.

When to Call a Professional

Natural methods handle most everyday pests, but some situations need a professional. Call one if you find signs of termites, a wasp nest inside the house, bed bugs, or a rodent problem that keeps returning after a few weeks of trapping. When you call, ask for a company that offers low-toxicity or integrated pest management options, and ask exactly what products they will use and where. A good company will start with inspection, sealing, and non-chemical methods before reaching for sprays.

The Bottom Line

Natural pest control is mostly good housekeeping: seal the gaps, store food properly, keep things dry, and use a few simple repellents. Most pests will simply go elsewhere, and your home stays free of chemical sprays. Start with one room this week — seal a gap, tidy a cupboard — and build from there.
