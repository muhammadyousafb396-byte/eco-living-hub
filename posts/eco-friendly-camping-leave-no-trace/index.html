Eco-Friendly Camping: A Beginner Guide to Leave No Trace

Camping is one of the greenest holidays you can take — no hotel, no air conditioning, just you and the outdoors. But a careless campsite can leave litter, damaged ground and dirty water behind. Eco-friendly camping is not about roughing it or buying expensive gear; it is about packing thoughtfully, cooking simply and leaving your pitch exactly as you found it. Anyone can do it, even on a first trip.

Pack Reusable, Not Disposable

The biggest source of camping waste is single-use packaging, so pack reusable gear instead. A refillable water bottle, a reusable coffee cup, a set of plates and cutlery, and cloth bags replace a whole bin bag of disposables — and they work better, too. Choose sturdy tent and sleeping gear that lasts for years rather than the cheapest set, and borrow or hire gear for your first trip instead of buying everything new. Pack food in reusable containers and beeswax wraps rather than cling film and foil.

Choose Your Campsite With Care

Where you camp matters as much as how you camp. Choose an established campsite or a marked pitch rather than a fragile meadow, and stay on paths and hard ground so you do not crush plants or disturb nesting birds. If you wild camp where it is allowed, pick a spot that has been used before and camp at least 60 metres from lakes and streams, so soap and waste never reach the water. Check the site rules before you go: some places ban fires, and some ask you to carry out all waste.

Cook Low-Waste Meals Outdoors

Meals are easy to plan with almost no waste. Cook simple one-pot meals from ingredients you prepared at home: chopped vegetables, cooked grains and marinated protein in reusable tubs, plus bread, fruit and nuts for snacks. Avoid individually wrapped snacks and tiny sauce sachets — decant sauces and oil into small reusable bottles instead. Bring a small washing-up bowl, biodegradable soap and a cloth, and wash up at least 60 metres from any stream, scattering the water widely so the soil filters it. For packed-lunch ideas, our zero-waste picnic guide has recipes and packing tips that work for campsites too.

Campfires: Enjoy Them Without Harming the Site

A campfire is part of camping for many people, but it needs care. Use an existing fire ring or a raised fire bowl, never build a new fire scar on fresh grass, and check whether fires are allowed at all — in dry summers and protected areas they are often banned. Burn only small, dead wood you find on the ground, never break branches off living trees, and keep the fire small. Let it burn down completely, then scatter the cold ashes widely.

Washing and Toilet Habits Outdoors

Even washing needs a little thought outdoors. Use biodegradable soap sparingly and wash away from streams and lakes, so the soil filters the water before it reaches them. For the toilet, use the campsite facilities when they exist. If there are none, dig a small hole 15 to 20 centimetres deep at least 60 metres from water, paths and camp, and bury waste properly. Carry out all toilet paper and hygiene products in a sealed bag — they do not break down quickly, and animals dig them up.

Wildlife: Watch, Do Not Feed

Feeding wild animals seems kind but does real harm: it teaches them to depend on people, spreads disease, and brings them into conflict with the next campers. Store all food in sealed containers or hung from a tree at night, and never leave food scraps around the tent. Keep a respectful distance from wildlife, and keep noise down at night so animals can feed and rest. If you camp with a dog, keep it under control — our guide to zero-waste pet care has tips for taking pets outdoors responsibly.

Your Leave No Trace Checklist

Before you leave, walk your pitch twice and check for every small thing: tent pegs, bottle tops, food wrappers, hair ties and cigarette ends. Pack out all rubbish, including food scraps and orange peel, which can take months to rot. Leave rocks, plants and natural souvenirs where they are. The rule is simple: take only photographs, leave only footprints.

Camp Light, Leave Nothing

Eco-friendly camping is really just thoughtful camping: reusable gear, simple food, a small fire or a stove, and a pitch left cleaner than you found it. Start with one trip using what you already own, borrow the rest, and add one greener habit each time you go.
