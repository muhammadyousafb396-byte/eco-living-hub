Low-VOC Paint: A Beginner's Guide to Healthier Walls

Painting a room is one of the quickest ways to make a home feel new, but the smell that comes with it can linger for days — and that smell is a sign of something worth paying attention to. Most conventional paints release chemicals called volatile organic compounds, or VOCs, into the air as they dry. Low-VOC paint gives you the same fresh colour with far fewer of those chemicals, so the room is healthier to live in while you paint and long after the paint has dried.

What Are VOCs and Why Do They Matter?

VOCs are chemicals that turn into gas at room temperature. In paint, they help it spread smoothly and dry evenly, but as the paint dries they escape into the air you breathe. In a freshly painted room, VOCs can cause headaches, eye irritation and dizziness, and they are worse for children, people with asthma and anyone who spends a lot of time indoors. Paint is also a major source of indoor air pollution: a freshly painted room can release VOCs for weeks after the job is done. Choosing a low-VOC paint is one of the simplest ways to cut that pollution at its source.

Low-VOC vs Zero-VOC: What the Labels Mean

Low-VOC paint contains less than 50 grams of VOCs per litre, while zero-VOC paint contains less than 5 grams per litre. There are two catches: the colourants added to tint the paint can contain VOCs of their own, so a deep colour mixed into a zero-VOC base may no longer be zero-VOC, and rules differ by country. Choose the lowest-VOC paint you can find in the colour you want, and ask the shop whether the tinting adds VOCs before you buy.

How to Choose Low-VOC Paint

Start by looking for a clear VOC figure on the tin, not just the words eco or green on the front. A tin that states its grams per litre is giving you real information; a tin that only says natural or environmentally friendly may not be. Water-based paints, also called latex or acrylic paints, are almost always lower in VOCs than oil-based paints, and they are easier to clean up with soap and water. For bedrooms and children's rooms, a zero-VOC paint is worth the small extra cost, because you sleep in that air every night. For kitchens and bathrooms, look for a low-VOC paint made for damp rooms, which resists mould without harsh chemicals. If you are unsure, buy a small sample pot first and test the colour and the smell in the room itself.

Prepare the Room and Paint Safely

Good preparation makes a low-VOC paint job even healthier. Move furniture out of the room or into the centre and cover it with old sheets. Clean the walls with warm soapy water and let them dry, because paint sticks better to a clean surface. Open the windows and, if you have one, put a fan in the window blowing air out of the room — even with low-VOC paint, fresh air helps it dry well and clears any smell fast. Wear old clothes and gloves, and if you are sanding old paint, wear a mask and check first whether the old paint could contain lead, which is a job for a professional in older homes. Keep children and pets out of the room until the paint is dry and the smell has gone.

Painting Tips for a Smooth Finish

Low-VOC paints work a little differently from old-style paints, so a few small adjustments help. Stir the paint well before you start, and pour only as much as you need into a tray. Use a roller for large wall areas and a brush for edges and corners, and work in small sections so each new patch blends into the one before it. Two thin coats almost always look better than one thick coat, and low-VOC paint often needs a little longer between coats, so follow the drying time on the tin. Clean brushes and rollers with warm soapy water straight after you finish, and seal the tin tightly if you have paint left over. Store leftover paint in a cool, dry place, and write the room and colour on the lid so you can touch up later.

A Fresher Room, a Healthier Home

Choosing low-VOC paint is a small decision that changes the air you breathe every day. The room looks just as good, the smell clears faster, and you have not filled your home with chemicals that linger for weeks. Start with one room — a bedroom is a good choice — and see how easy it is. For more ways to make your home healthier, see our indoor plants for cleaner air and our eco-friendly bedroom swaps.
