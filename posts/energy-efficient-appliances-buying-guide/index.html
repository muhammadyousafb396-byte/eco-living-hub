Energy-Efficient Appliances: A Simple Buying Guide

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-11-01 · Category: Energy and Water Saving

A new fridge, washing machine or oven is one of the biggest purchases in a home, and it keeps using energy every day for ten years or more. An efficient model can lower your energy bills for years, while a cheap, inefficient one can cost far more in electricity than it saved at the till. This guide explains how to read the energy label, pick the right size, and decide which features are worth paying for, so your next appliance is good for both your bills and your home.

Start with the biggest energy users

Not all appliances matter equally. The fridge runs all day and night, so it is usually the single biggest appliance energy user in a home. The washing machine and dryer, the dishwasher and the oven follow, depending on how often you use them. If you are replacing only one appliance, start with the one that runs longest or is oldest, because that is where efficiency pays back fastest. An old fridge can use far more electricity than a modern efficient model of the same size. Small daily habits matter too, and our guide to saving energy at home covers twenty simple changes that cut bills without buying anything new.

How to read the energy label

Every new appliance carries an energy label, and learning to read it takes two minutes. In the United States, look for the yellow EnergyGuide label, which shows estimated yearly electricity use in kilowatt-hours and an estimated yearly running cost. In the UK and European Union, look for the A to G energy rating, with A the most efficient, plus the yearly energy use figure. In both systems, the kilowatt-hour figure is the most useful number, because it lets you compare two models directly. A fridge using 250 kilowatt-hours a year costs roughly half as much to run as one using 500, whatever the brand. Compare models of a similar size and type.

Choose the right size, not the biggest

Bigger appliances use more energy, even efficient ones, so buy the size your household actually needs. A fridge that is too large cools empty space all day, and an oversized washing machine tempts you to run half-empty loads. For fridges, allow enough space for your normal weekly shop with a little room to spare, not enough for a party you host twice a year. For washing machines, match the drum size to your usual load: a household of one or two people rarely needs the largest drum on the market. For ovens, consider how you really cook. If you mostly cook for two, a smaller oven or a good combination microwave oven may serve you better than a huge double oven. Right-sizing is the simplest efficiency win there is, and it often saves money on the purchase price too.

Features worth paying for, and features to skip

Manufacturers love extra features, but only some save energy. Worth having: inverter compressors in fridges, heat-pump dryers, and eco modes you actually select. Question: ice and water dispensers, which add energy use, and always-on smart features. Ask one question about each feature: does it reduce energy or water use, or just add convenience? The same logic applies elsewhere in the home: efficient LED lighting at home uses far less energy than older bulbs, and comparing running costs is the habit that saves money everywhere.

Consider secondhand and refurbished models

The most sustainable appliance is often one that already exists. A good-quality secondhand fridge or washing machine only a few years old can be a smart buy from a reputable refurbisher who offers a warranty. Check the energy label before buying secondhand, and avoid very old models, whose energy use can wipe out the saving on the price. If you buy new, ask the retailer about take-back and recycling for your old appliance.

Use your new appliance efficiently

Even the most efficient appliance wastes energy if used badly. Keep your fridge at the temperature the manual recommends, usually around 3 to 5 degrees, and do not set it colder than needed. Wash clothes at lower temperatures with full loads, and run the dishwasher only when full. In the kitchen, matching pan size to hob ring size and putting lids on pots cuts cooking energy noticeably, as explained in our guide to energy-saving cooking habits. Use the eco programme as your default, not the fastest or hottest setting, and the savings your efficient purchase promised will actually arrive.

The bottom line

Buying an energy-efficient appliance comes down to three steps: compare the kilowatt-hour figure on the label, buy the right size for your household, and choose features that genuinely save energy. Do that, and your new appliance will quietly save you money for years. Look past the price tag and check the label first.
