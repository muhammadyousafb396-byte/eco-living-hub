Grow Herbs Indoors: Easy Windowsill Herb Garden for Beginners

Fresh herbs transform everyday cooking. A handful of basil lifts a tomato salad, mint freshens a drink, and parsley brightens a simple pasta dish. Store-bought herbs are costly, plastic-wrapped, and wilt fast, so growing your own solves all three problems. A windowsill herb garden costs very little to start, needs only a sunny spot and a few minutes of care a week, and gives you fresh herbs all year round. If you have never grown anything before, herbs are the perfect first plants.

Why Grow Herbs Indoors?

Indoor herbs are forgiving, fast-growing, and genuinely useful. Unlike many houseplants, they reward you with something you can eat. A small pot of basil supplies leaves for months, and one mint plant produces more than a household can use. A starter plant costs about the same as one store packet, but keeps producing for months, cutting packaging waste and saving money. Best of all, herbs grow happily on a kitchen windowsill, so your ingredients are always within arm's reach while you cook.

Step 1: Pick a Sunny Spot

Herbs need light more than anything else. A south-facing windowsill is ideal, but any bright spot that gets four to six hours of sunlight a day will work for most herbs. Watch your windowsill for a day and note where the sun falls. If your kitchen is short on light, a small grow light, or even a bright white LED bulb left on for a few hours in the evening, can make a big difference. Turn pots regularly so they grow evenly, and in winter keep them near the glass but off radiators, which dry soil fast.

Step 2: Choose the Easiest Herbs First

Start with herbs that are hard to kill and quick to use. These five are the best for beginners:

  • Basil. Loves sun and warmth, grows fast, and pairs with tomatoes, pasta, and salads. Pinch the top leaves regularly to keep it bushy.
  • Mint. Nearly impossible to kill, but grow it in its own pot because it spreads aggressively and will take over any shared container.
  • Parsley. Tolerates less light than basil, grows steadily for months, and works in almost every savory dish.
  • Chives. Mild onion flavor, very hardy, and regrows quickly after cutting. Snip with scissors instead of pulling.
  • Thyme. Drought-tolerant and compact, perfect for small pots and sunny sills. It needs less water than the others.

Buy starter plants first: they give usable leaves within days, while seeds take weeks. For a bigger project later, see our guide to growing food on a balcony.

Step 3: Pots, Soil, and Watering

Herbs need pots with drainage holes. Without drainage, roots sit in water and rot, which is the most common reason indoor herbs die. Terracotta pots are ideal because they breathe and dry out evenly, but any pot with holes and a saucer will work. Use regular potting mix, not garden soil, which compacts in containers. Water when the top inch of soil feels dry, watering thoroughly until a little drains, then empty the saucer. Most herbs prefer slightly dry soil, so when in doubt, wait a day. Feed lightly every four to six weeks. If you want free fertilizer for your pots, learn how to start composting at home, even in an apartment.

Step 4: Harvest the Right Way

Regular picking keeps herbs healthy and productive. Always cut from the top of the plant, just above a pair of leaves, which encourages two new branches to grow in its place. Never strip more than a third of a plant at once, and let it recover for a week before picking again. Harvest in the morning, when the leaves are most fragrant, and use scissors for clean cuts that heal quickly. If a plant starts flowering, pinch the flowers off, because flowering shifts energy away from leaf growth and can make the flavor bitter. Basil is especially quick to flower in warm rooms, so check it weekly.

Common Problems and Quick Fixes

Yellow leaves usually mean overwatering, so let soil dry out. Leggy, pale growth means too little light. Tiny flies are fungus gnats, cleared by letting the top layer dry. If a plant looks unhappy, cut it back by half, check drainage, and give it a week; herbs often bounce back.

The Bottom Line

A windowsill herb garden is the easiest way to start growing your own food. Pick a sunny spot, start with three forgiving herbs, water only when soil is dry, and harvest a little and often. Within weeks you will cook with your own herbs, spend less, and throw away less packaging.
