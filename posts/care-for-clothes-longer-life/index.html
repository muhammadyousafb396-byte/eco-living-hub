How to Make Your Clothes Last Longer: Simple Care Habits That Save Money

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-10-29 · Category: Sustainable Fashion

On this page

  • Wash Less, and Wash Cooler
  • Dry Gently and Skip the Tumble Dryer When You Can
  • Fix Small Damage Straight Away
  • Store Clothes So They Keep Their Shape
  • Buy Better, and Buy Less Often

The most sustainable item of clothing is usually the one you already own. Clothes wear out fastest not from being worn, but from being washed too hot, tumble-dried too hard and stored badly. A few gentle habits can add years to a favourite jumper, shirt or pair of jeans, and they cost nothing. This guide covers the simple care routines that keep clothes looking good for longer.

Wash Less, and Wash Cooler

Most everyday clothes do not need washing after every wear. Jeans, jumpers, jackets and trousers can often be aired, spot-cleaned or worn again before they need a full wash. When you do wash, use a cool cycle and a gentle detergent, and turn clothes inside out to protect prints and colours. Always read the care label first, especially for wool and delicate fabrics, and wash similar colours and fabrics together. The gentler approach in our eco-friendly laundry routine also saves water and energy, so your clothes and your bills both benefit.

Dry Gently and Skip the Tumble Dryer When You Can

High heat is hard on elastic, prints and natural fibres. Air-drying on a rack or line keeps clothes in better shape and uses no electricity at all. Reshape jumpers while they are damp and lay them flat to dry so they do not stretch out of shape. Hang shirts on hangers to dry and they will need far less ironing. If you must use a dryer, choose a low heat setting, take clothes out while they are slightly damp, and avoid over-drying, which makes fabrics feel rough and wears them out sooner.

Fix Small Damage Straight Away

A loose button, a small hole or a dropped hem is a ten-minute job when you catch it early, and a ruined garment when you leave it. Keep a small sewing kit with a needle, thread in a few neutral colours, spare buttons and safety pins. Learn two basics: sewing on a button and closing a small seam. For bigger jobs, a local tailor or repair service can replace a zip, take in a waistband or re-sole shoes for much less than buying new. Keeping a repair pile by the door, and dealing with it once a month, stops damaged clothes from sitting forgotten at the back of the wardrobe.

Store Clothes So They Keep Their Shape

Good storage prevents most wardrobe damage. Use sturdy hangers for jackets, shirts and dresses, fold heavy knitwear instead of hanging it, and give clothes breathing room so they do not crease or snag. Keep shoes clean and dry, stuff boots lightly to hold their shape, and rotate your shoes so no pair is worn every day. Store out-of-season clothes clean, dry and in breathable bags or boxes, away from damp and direct sunlight. Moths love natural fibres, so keep wardrobes clean and check stored clothes when the seasons change.

Buy Better, and Buy Less Often

Care habits work best when you start with clothes that are made to last. Before buying, check the seams, the fabric and the fastenings, and ask whether the item suits the life you actually live. A smaller wardrobe of well-chosen pieces, like the one in our sustainable capsule wardrobe guide, is easier to care for than a crowded one. Secondhand can be a smart choice too, as our thrift store shopping guide explains, because well-made older clothes have already proved they can last. When something finally reaches the end of its life, cut it into cleaning cloths or take it to a textile recycling point rather than binning it. Look after what you own, and it will look after you for years.
