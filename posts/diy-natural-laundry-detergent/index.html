DIY Natural Laundry Detergent: A Simple Recipe That Works

Laundry detergent is one of those household products we buy on autopilot, usually in a big plastic bottle, usually without reading the label. But most of what a detergent needs to do is simple: lift dirt, soften water, and leave clothes smelling fresh. Three basic ingredients can do all of that for pennies a load, with no plastic jug and no harsh chemicals. Making your own takes about ten minutes, and it is one of the easiest homemade swaps you can try.

Why Make Your Own Detergent?

The case for homemade detergent is practical, not just green. A standard bottle of detergent costs several dollars and lasts a few weeks, while the ingredients for a homemade batch cost a fraction of that and last for months. You also cut plastic waste, since the ingredients come in cardboard boxes and paper bags rather than single-use jugs. Finally, you know exactly what is in your detergent, which matters if anyone in your household has sensitive skin. It pairs well with the habits in our eco-friendly laundry routine, which covers water, energy, and drying.

Recipe: Simple Powder Detergent

This powder recipe is the easiest place to start. It works in standard and high-efficiency machines, and one batch lasts for months.

  • 1 cup washing soda: softens water and lifts dirt and grease. Find it in the laundry aisle, not to be confused with baking soda.
  • 1 cup baking soda: deodorizes clothes and boosts cleaning power.
  • 1 bar of castile soap, finely grated: the cleaning agent that lifts grime from fabric.
  • Optional: 10 to 15 drops of essential oil such as lavender or lemon, for a light, natural scent.

Mix everything in a large bowl, breaking up any clumps of grated soap as you go. Store the powder in a glass jar with a tight lid and label it clearly. Use one to two tablespoons per load, adding it to the drum before the clothes go in.

Recipe: Liquid Laundry Detergent

If you prefer a liquid, this version is just as simple and dissolves instantly, even in cold water. Dissolve 1 cup of washing soda and 1 cup of borax (optional, but helpful for hard water) in 4 litres of hot water in a large bucket. Melt 1 grated bar of castile soap into 2 litres of hot water on the stove, then stir it into the bucket. Let the mixture cool and pour it into clean, reused bottles or jars. It will thicken into a gel as it cools, which is normal.

Use about half a cup per load, shaking the bottle gently before pouring. The gel loosens with shaking, so it pours easily. One batch makes around 6 litres, which covers roughly 40 to 50 loads.

How Much to Use and How It Works

Homemade detergent is less concentrated than commercial formulas, so measuring matters. Too little leaves clothes dull, and too much leaves residue. Start with the amounts above and adjust by one spoonful at a time until clothes come out clean and rinse clear. Adding half a cup of white vinegar to the rinse cycle softens clothes naturally and helps remove any soap residue, so you can skip fabric softener entirely.

What Homemade Detergent Can and Cannot Do

Be honest about the limits. This detergent cleans everyday laundry very well, but it is not a stain remover. Pre-treat stains with a paste of baking soda and water, or a dab of liquid castile soap, before washing. It also has no optical brighteners, so very white shirts may look slightly less bright than with a commercial detergent. If your clothes feel stiff after washing, you are probably using too much detergent, so cut the amount and add vinegar to the rinse.

How Much Money Does It Save?

A batch of the powder recipe costs roughly two to three dollars in ingredients and covers about 60 loads, which works out to around five cents a load. A typical bottle of commercial detergent costs 10 to 15 dollars for 30 to 40 loads, or roughly 30 to 40 cents a load. A household doing five loads a week could save well over 50 dollars a year by switching.

Storing and Safety Tips

Store your detergent in a labelled, sealed container away from children and pets. Washing soda and borax are mild irritants, so handle them with dry hands and keep the powder out of reach. If you enjoy making your own household products, try our five DIY natural cleaning recipes next.

The Bottom Line

DIY laundry detergent is cheap, simple, and surprisingly effective. Three ingredients, ten minutes, and a jar are all it takes to stop buying plastic jugs of detergent. Start with the powder recipe, use the right amount for your water, and give it a few washes before judging the results.
