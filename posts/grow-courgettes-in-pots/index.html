Grow Courgettes in Pots: A Beginner Guide to a Big Harvest

Courgettes are the most generous vegetable a beginner can grow. One plant in a single pot can produce courgettes all summer long — often more than a household can eat. They grow fast and reward you with big yellow flowers and glossy green fruit within weeks of planting.

Why Courgettes Love Pots

Courgettes grow big and hungry, and a pot gives you control over the rich soil and steady water they need. A large container warms up quickly in spring, which helps young plants grow fast, and you can place it in the sunniest spot you have. One plant needs one big pot, so start with a single plant — most families find one is plenty. For more ideas on making the most of a small growing area, see our guide to growing vegetables in a small space.

Choosing the Right Pot and Soil

Pick the biggest pot you can manage — at least 40 litres, about 45 centimetres wide and deep — with drainage holes in the bottom. Courgettes drink a lot of water, so a large pot holds moisture better and means less daily watering. Fill it with rich multi-purpose compost mixed with a bucket of well-rotted manure or garden compost if you have it. Courgettes are hungry feeders, and rich soil is the secret to a big harvest. Set the pot on bricks or pot feet so excess water drains away freely.

How to Sow Courgette Seeds

Start seeds indoors in mid to late spring, about three to four weeks before the last frost in your area. Sow one seed per small pot, about two centimetres deep, on its side to stop it rotting. Keep the pots warm — a sunny windowsill works well — and seedlings usually appear within a week. Once they have two or three true leaves, move them into their final big pot. Wait until nights are reliably warm before putting plants outside for good, hardening them off over a few days first.

Watering and Feeding

Water courgettes generously and consistently, aiming at the soil rather than the leaves. In hot weather, a big pot can need water every day, sometimes twice. The soil should stay damp but never waterlogged. Once the first flowers appear, feed the plant every week with a tomato feed or any balanced liquid fertiliser. Courgettes produce separate male and female flowers, and bees usually do the pollinating. If fruits form but then rot at the tip, pollination has failed — brush a male flower's centre against a female flower, which is the one with a tiny courgette behind it.

Common Problems and Easy Fixes

Courgettes are tough, but a few problems do appear. Powdery mildew shows up as white patches on leaves in late summer but rarely harms the harvest. Slugs love young plants, so protect seedlings with a ring of crushed eggshells around the pot. If leaves turn yellow, the plant is usually thirsty or hungry — water deeply and feed it. Aphids sometimes cluster on new growth; wash them off with a strong jet of water. Remove any yellowing leaves at the base to keep air moving through the plant.

Harvesting Your Courgettes

Courgettes grow fast — a small fruit can double in size overnight, so check plants every day or two once they start fruiting. Pick them when they are about 15 to 20 centimetres long, while the skin is glossy and the flesh is tender. Use a sharp knife or scissors to cut them from the plant, leaving a short stalk. Picking regularly is the key to a long harvest: the more you pick, the more the plant produces. Leave a courgette too long and it becomes a marrow — edible, but watery and less tasty.

The Bottom Line

Growing courgettes in pots is one of the most rewarding things a beginner gardener can do. A big pot, rich soil, regular water, and a sunny spot are all it takes, and a single plant will keep your kitchen supplied all summer. Start with one plant this year and see how generous it is.
