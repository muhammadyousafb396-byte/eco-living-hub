Eco-Friendly Oven Cleaning: A Simple Guide Without Harsh Chemicals

A dirty oven is one of those jobs everyone puts off. Grease bakes on, spills turn to black crust, and the usual answer is a can of harsh oven cleaner full of fumes and warnings. The good news is that you do not need it. A simple paste of baking soda and water, plus a little patience, cleans most ovens just as well, with no chemical smell and nothing harsh left behind on the surfaces where your food cooks. This guide shows you the method step by step.

What You Need

You probably have them already: baking soda, water, white vinegar in a spray bottle, a bowl, an old toothbrush or small brush, a sponge or cloth, and rubber gloves. A plastic scraper or an old credit card helps with the thickest baked-on patches. Avoid steel wool and harsh scouring pads, which can scratch enamel and glass. Lay an old towel on the floor to catch drips, and make sure the oven is completely cool before you start.

The Baking Soda Paste Method

Start by removing the oven racks and any loose crumbs. Mix about half a cup of baking soda with a few tablespoons of water to make a thick, spreadable paste — like toothpaste. Spread it over the inside surfaces of the oven, including the bottom, sides, and door, but keep it away from the heating elements. Leave the paste to work for at least 12 hours, or overnight if you can. The baking soda slowly loosens baked-on grease while you get on with your day. For more ideas, our guide to natural cleaning for every surface covers wood, glass, steel, and stone.

Wipe, Spray, and Rinse

The next day, wipe out as much of the dried paste as you can with a damp cloth. It will come away brown and greasy — that is a good sign. Then spray the inside lightly with white vinegar. The vinegar reacts with any baking soda left behind and fizzes, lifting the last of the grime. Wipe everything again with a clean damp cloth, rinsing the cloth often, until the surfaces feel smooth and no residue remains. For stubborn patches that survive the first pass, make a fresh small batch of paste, leave it for an hour, and scrub gently with the toothbrush. A plastic scraper lifts thick crusts without scratching.

Cleaning the Racks and Glass Door

Soak the racks in the bathtub or a large sink with hot water and a few tablespoons of baking soda, or washing-up liquid, for a few hours. Then scrub them with a brush and rinse. For the glass door, spread a thin layer of baking soda paste on the inside of the glass, leave it for 30 minutes, and wipe clean. If your oven door has a removable inner glass panel, follow the manual to take it out — grease often hides between the panes. A simple paste of baking soda and water is also the base of many of the recipes in our DIY natural cleaning products guide, which is worth a look if you want to make more of your own cleaners.

Dealing With Stubborn Grease and Smells

Old grease that has been baked on for months may need two rounds of the paste method, and that is normal. A paste made with baking soda and a little washing-up liquid adds extra grease-cutting power for the worst spots. For lingering smells, wipe the oven with a cloth dampened with water and a splash of vinegar, then heat the empty oven to 100°C for ten minutes with the door slightly open to air it out. Placing a small bowl of baking soda inside the oven overnight between cleans absorbs odours. Never mix vinegar with bleach, and never use bleach in the oven — the heat and food contact make it unsafe.

Keeping Your Oven Clean for Longer

Once your oven is clean, keeping it that way takes very little effort. Wipe up spills as soon as the oven is cool enough to touch, before they bake into crust. Line the bottom shelf with a reusable silicone oven liner or a sheet of foil to catch drips — never cover the oven floor itself, as that blocks airflow. A quick wipe with a damp cloth once a week stops grease building up, and a deep clean every two to three months keeps the job small. Small habits like these add up across the whole kitchen; our guide to eco-friendly dishwasher habits shows how the same approach saves water and energy at the sink. With baking soda, water, and a little patience, your oven can be spotless without a single harsh chemical.
