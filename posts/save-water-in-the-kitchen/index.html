Save Water in the Kitchen: Simple Habits That Cut Your Bill

The kitchen uses more water than any other room except the bathroom, and much of it goes straight down the drain. Washing vegetables, rinsing dishes, boiling pasta, and running the tap while you cook all add up — and every litre costs money twice, once to supply it and once to heat it. The good news is that saving water in the kitchen rarely means doing without. It mostly means changing small habits and catching water you would otherwise waste.

Stop Running the Tap

The single biggest kitchen water saver is also the simplest: turn the tap off when you are not using it. Washing vegetables under a running tap uses far more water than filling a bowl. Rinsing dishes under a running tap does the same. Fill a basin or bowl instead, and you will use a fraction of the water for the same result. A household that breaks the running-tap habit can save thousands of litres a year without noticing any difference.

Catch and Reuse the Water You Already Use

Much of the water used in the kitchen is clean enough to use again. The water you rinse vegetables in is perfect for watering houseplants or the garden. Pasta water, once cooled, is full of starch and makes a good plant feed. The water you run while waiting for the tap to heat up can be caught in a jug and used for the kettle or for watering plants. Keep a jug or bowl by the sink as a reminder. Even the water from a dehumidifier or a drying rack drip tray can go on the garden. Once you start catching it, you will be surprised how much water you were throwing away.

Wash Dishes the Water-Smart Way

How you wash dishes makes a big difference. A dishwasher, when run full on an eco setting, usually uses less water than washing the same load by hand under a running tap. If you wash by hand, fill one side of the sink with hot soapy water and the other with clean rinse water, instead of rinsing each item under the tap. Scrape food off plates instead of rinsing them before loading the dishwasher — modern machines handle the rest. Soak stubborn pots for a few minutes rather than scrubbing them under running water. For a full guide to water-smart dishwashing, see our eco-friendly dishwashing routine.

Cook With Less Water

Cooking itself offers easy savings. Steam vegetables instead of boiling them — it uses less water and keeps more nutrients in the food. Use just enough water to cover pasta or potatoes rather than filling the pot to the top. Keep a lid on pots so water does not evaporate, which also cooks food faster and saves energy. Reuse cooking water where it makes sense: the water from steaming vegetables can become the base for soup or stock, and pasta water adds flavour and body to sauces. These small changes save water and often make the food taste better too.

Fix Leaks and Drips

A dripping kitchen tap can waste a surprising amount of water — a single drip per second adds up to thousands of litres over a year. Check taps, the dishwasher connection, and the pipes under the sink for drips or damp patches, and fix them promptly. A worn washer is usually the cause and costs very little to replace. If your tap has no aerator, fit one: it mixes air into the water stream, so the flow feels just as strong while using less water. The same idea applies to the bathroom — see our guide to saving water in the shower for more ways to cut water use around the home.

Choose Water-Smart Appliances and Habits

When buying a dishwasher or washing machine, look for a good water-efficiency rating and use the eco programme whenever you can. Run full loads only — a half-empty dishwasher uses almost as much water as a full one. Keep a jug of water in the fridge instead of running the tap until it goes cold, and use a kettle with a water gauge so you only boil what you need. Teach the whole household the new habits: a sticker by the sink reminding everyone to turn off the tap helps until it becomes automatic.

The Bottom Line

Saving water in the kitchen is not about sacrifice. It is about using a bowl instead of a running tap, catching water you would otherwise waste, washing dishes smartly, and fixing the odd drip. Together, these small habits cut your water use noticeably, lower your bills, and keep a precious resource from going down the drain.
