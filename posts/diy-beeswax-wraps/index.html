DIY Beeswax Wraps: Reusable Food Wrap You Can Make at Home

Cling film is used for a few minutes and lasts for centuries in landfill. Beeswax wraps do the same job, covering bowls, wrapping sandwiches and keeping food fresh, and they can be washed and reused for a year or more.

What You Need

The shopping list is short. You need 100 percent cotton fabric, beeswax pellets or a block of beeswax, and a baking tray lined with baking paper. Choose cotton in patterns you like, since the wraps will live in your kitchen and your lunchbox. Lightweight quilting cotton works best; avoid stretchy or synthetic fabric, because it will not absorb the wax evenly. A block of beeswax from a local beekeeper or craft shop is ideal, and a small amount goes a long way: about 100 grams of beeswax makes several wraps.

Make Your Wraps Step by Step

Start by washing and drying your fabric, then cut it into useful sizes: small squares around 20 centimetres for covering jars and half a lemon, medium squares around 28 centimetres for sandwiches and bowls, and large pieces around 35 centimetres for covering plates and loaves of bread. If you use pinking shears, the edges will not fray. Preheat your oven to a low heat, around 100 degrees Celsius. Lay a piece of fabric on the lined baking tray and sprinkle beeswax evenly over it, using a little more in the centre and less near the edges. Slide the tray into the oven for about five minutes, until the wax has melted. Take the tray out and brush the melted wax to the edges, then lift the fabric by a corner, hold it up for a few seconds while the wax sets, and hang it on a line or a chair back to cool. That is it: your first wrap is ready.

How to Use Beeswax Wraps

Using a beeswax wrap is simple. Warm it with your hands for a few seconds, then press it over a bowl or around food. The heat from your hands softens the wax just enough for it to stick to itself and to the edges of a dish. Use wraps for sandwiches, cheese, cut fruit and vegetables, bread, and covering bowls of leftovers. They are not suitable for raw meat or fish, and they should not go in the microwave or near a hot oven, since the wax will melt. Washing and Looking After Them

To wash a wrap, rinse it under cool water with a drop of mild washing-up liquid, then air-dry it on a rack. Never use hot water, which melts the wax, and never put wraps in the dishwasher or washing machine. With gentle care, a wrap lasts about a year, and you can tell it is wearing out when the wax coating feels thin and it stops sticking. At that point, you can refresh it by adding a little more beeswax and heating it in the oven again, just as you did when you made it. When a wrap is finally too worn to use, it can be composted, since cotton and beeswax both break down naturally.

How Much Money and Waste They Save

A roll of cling film seems cheap, but it is a cost that never ends, and every metre of it becomes waste. A set of homemade beeswax wraps costs a few pounds or dollars in materials and replaces roll after roll for a year. If your household uses cling film every day, switching to wraps means one less item on every shopping list and one less plastic product in your bin. The savings add up quietly, and the habit spreads: once you have wraps in the drawer, you start noticing other single-use items you can replace. For ideas on using up the food you wrap, see our guide to using up leftovers and reducing food waste.

Small Tweaks and Variations

Once you have the basic method, you can adapt it. Add a teaspoon of pine resin to the wax for a stickier wrap, or a little jojoba oil for a softer, more flexible finish. Both are optional and easy to find in craft shops. You can also make wraps in seasonal patterns for gifts: a set of three in cheerful fabric makes a thoughtful present for someone starting a lower-waste kitchen.

One Hour, a Year of Reuse

DIY beeswax wraps are a small project with an outsized payoff. In one afternoon you can replace a product you buy again and again with something you made yourself, and every time you reach for a wrap instead of a roll of plastic, you will feel the difference.
