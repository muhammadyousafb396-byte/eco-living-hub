Eco-Friendly Shoes: How to Choose Sustainable Footwear

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-10-29 · Category: Sustainable Fashion

On this page

  • Start With How You Use Your Shoes
  • Materials: What to Look For
  • Fit and Comfort Matter Most
  • Buy Less, Buy Second-Hand, Repair More

Shoes work harder than almost anything else you own. They carry you through rain, pavements, stairs, and long days on your feet, so it is no surprise that they wear out. Choosing sustainable footwear is not about buying a pair labelled green and hoping for the best. It is about finding shoes that fit well, suit how you actually live, and can be repaired or resoled when they start to wear. A comfortable pair that lasts for several seasons will usually have a smaller impact than several cheaper pairs that hurt your feet and fall apart.

Start With How You Use Your Shoes

Before you shop, think about where your shoes go. Everyday shoes for walking, work, school runs, and errands need comfort and grip. Work shoes may need to look smart and stay comfortable for long hours standing. Trainers for sport need support and cushioning suited to the activity. One pair rarely does every job well, but most people do not need a large collection either. Two or three well-chosen pairs, one smart, one casual, and one for active days, cover most weeks. If you are simplifying what you own, our guide to a sustainable capsule wardrobe can help you decide which shoes earn a place and which ones you can let go.

Materials: What to Look For

Shoe materials affect comfort, durability, and care. Leather, when well made and looked after, can last for many years and can often be resoled. It does need regular cleaning and conditioning, so only choose leather if you are willing to care for it. Canvas and cotton are light and breathable, easy to clean, and good for casual shoes, although they wear faster in wet weather. Cork, natural rubber, and recycled rubber are common in soles and can offer good grip and cushioning. Some brands now use recycled plastics, plant-based foams, or leather made from food-industry by-products. These can be good options, but check the whole shoe, not just one material claim. A shoe made partly from recycled material that falls apart quickly is not a better choice than a durable shoe made from conventional materials.

Check the Sole and Stitching

The sole is where many shoes fail first. Look for a sole that is stitched or well bonded to the upper, with a tread pattern that suits the surfaces you walk on. A sole that can be replaced by a cobbler is a strong advantage, especially for boots and smart shoes. Check stitching around the sole, heel, and toe. Neat, even stitching and no loose threads suggest careful making. Flex the shoe gently. It should bend near the ball of the foot, not in the middle, and it should spring back without creasing sharply.

Fit and Comfort Matter Most

No material or label matters if shoes hurt your feet. Shop later in the day if you can, when feet are slightly larger, and wear the socks you would normally wear with the shoes. Walk around, go up and down a step if possible, and stand still for a minute. Your toes should have room to move, your heel should not slip, and the widest part of your foot should line up with the widest part of the shoe. Shoes should feel comfortable straight away. A short breaking-in period is normal for stiff leather, but sharp pain, pinching, or rubbing that leaves a mark is a sign to try another size or style. If you need extra support, removable insoles let you add the insole that suits you without changing the shoe.

  • Try both shoes on, as feet are often slightly different sizes.
  • Leave a thumb-width of space between your longest toe and the front.
  • Check that the heel stays put when you walk.
  • Walk on a hard floor, not only on carpet.
  • Keep the receipt and wear shoes indoors first if returns are allowed.

Buy Less, Buy Second-Hand, Repair More

Second-hand shoes can be a good option for styles that are barely worn, such as dress shoes, boots, or children's shoes that were outgrown quickly. Check soles, heels, insoles, and stitching carefully, and clean second-hand shoes well before wearing them. For everyday shoes, buying new may make sense if you need a precise fit or specific support. When you do buy new, look for brands that offer repair, spare laces, or resoling, and keep the box and care instructions.
