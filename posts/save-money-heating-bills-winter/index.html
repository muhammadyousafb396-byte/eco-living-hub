Save Money on Heating Bills: Simple Winter Energy-Saving Habits

Heating is usually the biggest part of a household's winter energy bill. The good news is that you do not need an expensive new boiler or a full renovation to spend less. A few simple habits — blocking draughts, using your thermostat well, and heating only the rooms you use — can cut your heating bill noticeably while keeping your home just as comfortable. Here is how to start.

Why Heating Costs So Much in Winter

In a typical home, heating uses more energy than everything else combined in winter. Every gap around a door or window lets warm air out and cold air in, so your heating has to work harder to keep the same temperature. Older homes are often the worst, but even newer homes lose heat through thin spots — uninsulated floors, single glazing, and rooms that are heated when nobody is in them. The fix is rarely one big change. It is a handful of small ones that add up.

Block the Draughts

Draughts are the cheapest problem to fix and one of the biggest wins. Hold a lit candle or a strip of tissue near doors and windows on a windy day — if it flickers, you have found a draught. Self-adhesive foam strips cost very little and seal most window gaps in minutes, while a draught excluder along the bottom of a door stops cold air creeping in. Do not forget the letterbox, keyholes, and gaps around pipes. Our guide to draught-proofing your home for winter walks through each spot step by step.

Use Your Thermostat Wisely

Turning your thermostat down by just one degree can cut heating costs by a noticeable amount over a winter, and most people barely feel the difference. Aim for a comfortable temperature during the day — around 18 to 21 degrees — and lower it at night or when the house is empty. If you have a programmable thermostat, set heating to come on half an hour before you wake up and to switch off half an hour before bed. A smart thermostat can help with this, and our smart thermostat guide explains which features actually save energy and which are just nice to have.

Heat Only the Rooms You Use

There is no reason to heat a spare bedroom or a hallway to the same temperature as your living room. Close the doors of rooms you are not using, turn their radiators down, and let the rooms you live in stay warm. Curtains help too: open them on sunny days to let the sun warm the room, and close them as soon as it gets dark to keep the heat in. Thick curtains, or even a simple thermal lining, make a real difference on cold nights.

Look After Your Heating System

A heating system that is not looked after wastes energy. Bleed your radiators if the top feels cooler than the bottom — trapped air stops them heating properly, and bleeding takes five minutes with a radiator key. Keep furniture and curtains away from radiators so the heat can circulate, and put reflective foil behind radiators on outside walls to bounce heat back into the room. If your boiler is old, an annual service keeps it running efficiently and can catch small problems before they become expensive ones.

Free Habits That Keep You Warmer

Some of the best heating savings cost nothing at all. Wear a warm layer indoors instead of turning the heating up — a jumper is cheaper than a degree on the thermostat. Use a hot water bottle or a blanket in the evening rather than heating the whole house for one person. Cook with the oven door left open after baking to let the leftover heat into the kitchen, and dry clothes on a rack near a radiator instead of using a tumble dryer. Small habits like these add up over a long winter.

Read Your Bill and Track What You Save

It is hard to know if your changes are working unless you check. Read your energy bill each month and compare it with the same month last year — most suppliers show this on the bill or in their app. If your usage is falling, your habits are paying off. Our guide to reading your energy bill explains what each line means and how to spot waste.

Warm Home, Lower Bill

Saving money on heating is not about sitting in a cold house. It is about keeping the heat you pay for inside, using it where you need it, and letting simple habits do the rest. Start with the draughts and the thermostat this week, and by the end of winter you should see the difference — in your bill and in how comfortable your home feels.
