Save Water at Home: 15 Easy Ways to Use Less Water

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-10-27 · Category: Energy and Water Saving

Most of the water a household uses disappears in ways nobody notices: a long shower, a half-empty dishwasher, a tap left running while brushing teeth, or a garden hose watering the pavement. The good news is that saving water at home rarely means giving anything up. It mostly means paying a little more attention, fixing small leaks, and choosing a few smarter habits. Start with the ideas below, and your water bill — and your footprint — will both shrink.

Start in the Bathroom

The bathroom is where most household water goes, so it is the best place to begin. Shortening your shower by just two minutes can save a surprising amount over a year, especially in a family where everyone showers daily. If your shower is old, a low-flow showerhead is one of the cheapest upgrades you can make: modern designs feel just as strong while using much less water, and they pay for themselves through lower bills. Check for a dripping tap or a toilet that keeps running after flushing, because a slow leak can waste more water in a week than a family uses in a day.

Be Smarter in the Kitchen

  • Run full loads only. Wait until the dishwasher is full before switching it on, and use the eco cycle when you can. A full dishwasher usually uses less water than washing the same dishes by hand under a running tap.
  • Scrape, do not rinse. Scrape food into the bin or compost instead of rinsing plates under running water. If dishes need soaking, fill one side of the sink instead of letting the tap run.
  • Keep drinking water in the fridge. A jug of cold water in the fridge stops you running the tap until it turns cold, and it makes drinking water easy to reach.
  • Fix kitchen leaks quickly. A dripping kitchen tap wastes water around the clock. Replacing a washer is cheap, and it stops the waste the same day.

Laundry Habits That Save Water

Laundry uses a lot of water, but most of it is wasted on half loads and unnecessarily hot cycles. Always wash a full load, and choose cold water whenever the clothes are not heavily soiled — modern detergents clean well in cold water, and your clothes last longer too. Air-drying clothes, as described in our eco-friendly laundry routine, saves energy as well as the extra rinse cycles some dryers encourage. When it is time to replace a washing machine, look for a high-efficiency model.

Use Less Water Outside

Gardens and yards can use more water than the whole house in dry weather, so outdoor habits matter. Water plants early in the morning or in the evening, when less water evaporates, and water the soil rather than spraying leaves. Mulch around plants with bark, straw, or compost to hold moisture in the soil, which means you water less often. Collecting rainwater in a butt or barrel gives you free water for plants. If you grow herbs or salad leaves, our guide to growing food on a balcony shows how container plants can thrive with careful, targeted watering.

Low-Cost Fixes Worth Doing

A few small purchases can cut water use for years. A low-flow showerhead and tap aerators cost little, fit in minutes, and reduce flow without making showers feel weak. A dual-flush converter or a simple displacement device in an older toilet cistern reduces the water used per flush. A water butt collects rain for the garden. Pair them with the energy habits in our guide to saving energy at home, because heating water is one of the biggest energy costs in most homes — using less hot water saves both water and energy at once.

How to Track Your Progress

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Read your water meter once a week, at the same time, and write the number down. Within a month you will see your normal pattern, and you will notice quickly if a leak starts. Many water suppliers also show your usage on each bill, so compare this quarter with the same quarter last year. Set one simple goal at a time, such as shorter showers for a month, rather than trying to change everything at once. Small, steady changes are the ones that stick.

The Bottom Line

Saving water at home is not about discomfort or sacrifice. It is about fixing leaks, running full loads, watering wisely, and turning taps off when the water is not needed. Pick three ideas from this list today, and keep them going for a month. You will use noticeably less water, your bills should fall, and you will have built habits that help your household for years.
