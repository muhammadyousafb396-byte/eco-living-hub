Reusable Period Products: An Honest Beginner Guide

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-10-29 · Category: Zero Waste Living

  • How to Choose Your First Product
  • Washing and Caring for Reusables

Reusable period products can cut a lot of waste, save money over time, and feel more comfortable for many people — but choosing your first product can feel confusing. Cups, discs, cloth pads, and period underwear all work differently, and what suits one person may not suit another. This guide explains the main options honestly, without pressure or promises, so you can start slowly, try one product, and decide what works for your body, your flow, and your daily life. There is no single right choice, and disposable products are still a valid option whenever you need them.

The Main Reusable Options

Menstrual cups are small, flexible cups worn internally to collect flow. They are emptied, rinsed, and reinserted, and one cup can last for years with proper care. Many people like them for swimming, sport, and longer wear between changes, but insertion and removal take practice, and finding the right size and firmness can take a couple of cycles. Follow the maker's instructions carefully, wash hands before handling, and sterilise the cup as directed between periods.

Cloth Pads and Period Underwear

Cloth pads work like disposable pads but are washed and reused. They usually have a soft top layer, an absorbent core, and a waterproof backing, and they fasten with poppers around underwear. Many people find them comfortable and breathable, and they are a good first reusable option because they feel familiar. Period underwear looks and feels like regular underwear with built-in absorbent layers. It can be worn alone on lighter days or as backup with a cup or disc on heavier days. Both options need regular washing, so having several in rotation helps.

How to Choose Your First Product

Consider your budget, too. Reusable products cost more upfront than a pack of disposables, but they are designed to last. The budgeting ideas in sustainable shopping on a budget apply here: buy one product first, try it for two or three cycles, and only then buy more. Avoid buying a full set of everything before you know what you like. Check return policies and starter kits, and buy from reputable makers with clear care instructions.

Washing and Caring for Reusables

Good care keeps reusable products hygienic and long-lasting. Rinse cloth pads and period underwear in cold water as soon as you can, then wash them with your regular laundry in cold or warm water, without fabric softener, which can reduce absorbency. Air-dry them when possible, as high heat can damage waterproof layers. Store clean, dry products in a breathable bag or drawer, away from damp bathrooms.

For cups and discs, rinse with water between uses during your period, wash with a gentle, unscented soap as directed, and sterilise between periods by boiling or using a sterilising method recommended by the maker. Always follow the specific instructions for your product, as materials and guidance vary. The laundry habits in our eco-friendly laundry routine — cold washes, full loads, and air-drying — work well for cloth pads and period underwear too.

What About Comfort, Leaks, and Learning Curves?

Most reusable products take a little practice. Cups and discs may leak while you learn positioning, cloth pads may feel bulkier than disposables at first, and period underwear may need a backup pad on heavy days until you know its capacity. This is normal. Give yourself two or three cycles to learn, wear backup protection while you practise, and keep a spare product and a wet bag in your bag when you are out.

An Honest, Personal Choice

Reusable period products are not for everyone, and that is completely fine. Bodies, flows, lifestyles, and access to washing facilities all differ, and the best product is the one that works for you, reliably and comfortably. If you try a reusable option and like it, it can reduce waste and cost over time. If you prefer disposables, choose the products that suit you and dispose of them responsibly. Either way, an informed, unpressured choice — made at your own pace — is the sustainable one.
