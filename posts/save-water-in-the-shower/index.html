Save Water in the Shower: Simple Habits That Cut Your Water Bill

The shower is one of the biggest water users in most homes. A single ten-minute shower can use more water than a whole day of drinking, cooking, and hand-washing combined, and most of that water goes straight down the drain. The good news is that you can cut your shower water use by a third or more without giving up a warm, comfortable shower. Here is how.

How Much Water Does a Shower Really Use?

An older showerhead can use 15 litres of water per minute or more, which means a ten-minute shower uses around 150 litres. A modern low-flow showerhead uses about 7 to 9 litres per minute, so the same ten minutes uses closer to 80. That difference adds up fast: a household of four can save thousands of litres a year just by changing the showerhead and trimming a few minutes off each shower. The savings show up twice — on your water bill and your energy bill, because every litre of hot water you do not use is a litre you do not have to heat. If you want a full picture of where your household water goes, our guide to saving water at home walks through the whole house, room by room.

Shorten Your Shower Without Suffering

You do not need a cold, rushed shower to save water. Try a simple timer: aim for five minutes instead of ten, and use a phone timer or a small shower timer for the first week until the new routine feels normal. The easiest way to cut time is to turn the water off while you shampoo, soap up, or shave, then turn it back on to rinse. This one habit alone can save several litres per shower. Shorter showers are the single fastest way to cut water use, and they cost nothing at all.

Switch to a Low-Flow Showerhead

A low-flow showerhead is the best value water-saving upgrade in the bathroom. It mixes air into the water stream, so the spray still feels full and strong while using far less water. Most models fit a standard shower fitting and take about ten minutes to install. Look for a model with a flow rate under 9 litres per minute and an adjustable spray, so you can switch between a gentle rinse and a stronger jet. Once it is fitted, you will barely notice the difference — except on your bills. Our guide to water-saving bathroom fixtures covers taps and toilets too, so you can upgrade the whole bathroom in one go.

Fix Drips and Leaks Straight Away

A shower that drips after you turn it off is wasting water around the clock. The fix is usually a new washer that costs very little. Check the showerhead and the taps once a month: dry the fittings, wait a few minutes, and look for fresh drops. If the showerhead keeps dripping, soak it in vinegar overnight to clear limescale, then rinse and refit it. Small leaks look harmless, but they are the quietest way to waste water in your home.

Catch the Cold Water While You Wait

Most showers run cold for a few seconds before the hot water arrives, and that water usually goes straight down the drain. Put a bucket or a large jug under the showerhead while you wait, and use the caught water for something useful: water your houseplants, fill the toilet cistern, mop the floor, or soak laundry. A family can catch several litres a day this way. Keep a watering can by the shower so you actually use the water, and try the same trick at the kitchen sink while you wait for hot water.

Make It a Habit the Whole Household Follows

Water-saving habits work best when everyone in the house is on board. Talk about the goal as a family challenge rather than a rule: see who can take the shortest comfortable shower, or track the water meter together for a month and celebrate the drop. A shower timer or a small chart on the bathroom door can turn saving water into a friendly competition. If you wash clothes as well, our guide to an eco-friendly laundry routine shows how cooler washes and fuller loads save water and energy at the same time.

Start With One Change Today

You do not need to do everything at once. Pick one change — a shorter shower, a low-flow showerhead, or a bucket under the tap — and start today. Each change is small on its own, but together they can cut your shower water use by a third or more, lower your bills, and leave more water in the system for everyone. The best time to start is your next shower.
