DIY Natural Cleaning Products: 5 Recipes That Work

Walk down the cleaning aisle and you will find a different spray for every surface, each in its own plastic bottle, each costing several dollars. Most of those products are built from a handful of simple ingredients you already have at home. Making your own cleaners costs pennies per bottle, cuts plastic waste, and keeps harsh chemicals out of the air you breathe. These five recipes cover almost every cleaning job in a typical home.

Before You Start: Supplies and Safety

You only need a few basics: white vinegar, baking soda, liquid castile soap, washing soda, and a couple of glass spray bottles. Label every bottle clearly with its contents and the date you made it. A few safety rules matter. Never mix vinegar with bleach, and never mix vinegar with hydrogen peroxide in the same bottle. Vinegar can damage natural stone counters like marble and granite, so use a castile soap solution on those surfaces instead. Always test a new cleaner on a small hidden spot first.

Recipe 1: All-Purpose Cleaner

Mix one part white vinegar with one part water in a spray bottle, then add a few drops of dish soap. Spray on counters, sinks, and appliances, let it sit for a minute, and wipe with a cloth. This handles daily kitchen and bathroom cleaning. For a fresher smell, add a few drops of lemon or lavender essential oil. Cost: just a few cents per bottle.

Recipe 2: Glass and Mirror Cleaner

Combine two cups of water, half a cup of vinegar, and a quarter cup of rubbing alcohol in a spray bottle. Spray lightly on glass and mirrors and wipe with a microfiber cloth or old newspaper for a streak-free shine. This recipe dries fast and leaves no film, which is why it works better than many store-bought glass cleaners.

Recipe 3: Scrubbing Paste for Sinks and Tubs

Mix baking soda with a small amount of water until it forms a thick paste. Spread it on sinks, tubs, and tile grout, let it sit for ten minutes, then scrub with a brush and rinse. For extra power on soap scum, spray vinegar over the paste and let it fizz before scrubbing. This paste replaces abrasive chemical cleaners and costs almost nothing.

Recipe 4: Floor Cleaner

Add half a cup of vinegar and a few drops of castile soap to a bucket of warm water. Mop tile, laminate, and vinyl floors with this solution and let them air dry. For wood floors, skip the vinegar and use just warm water with a small amount of castile soap, wringing the mop well so the floor does not stay wet. One bucket cleans an entire home for pennies.

Recipe 5: Deodorizing Carpet and Fabric Freshener

Sprinkle baking soda generously over carpets, rugs, or upholstery and let it sit for at least thirty minutes, or overnight for strong odors. Vacuum thoroughly. Baking soda absorbs smells instead of covering them up, which makes it more effective than most scented sprays. For a light fragrance, mix a few drops of essential oil into the baking soda before sprinkling.

How Much Money Does This Save?

A household that buys separate cleaners for kitchens, bathrooms, glass, floors, and fabrics can easily spend 100 dollars or more per year on cleaning products. The ingredients for these five recipes cost a fraction of that and last for months. A gallon of vinegar, a box of baking soda, and a bottle of castile soap together cost less than twenty dollars and can supply a year's worth of homemade cleaners.

The Bottom Line

Homemade cleaners are cheaper, simpler, and gentler than most store-bought products, and they work just as well for everyday cleaning. Start with the all-purpose cleaner, keep a labeled spray bottle under the sink, and expand to the other recipes as you run out of commercial products. Your home will be clean, your air will be fresher, and your wallet will thank you.
