Eco-Friendly Travel: How to Plan a Lower-Waste Holiday

By Eco Living Hub · Published 2026-11-04 · Category: Sustainable Lifestyle

  • Choose Where You Stay

  • Leave Places Better Than You Found Them

A holiday can create a surprising amount of waste: single-use toiletries, plastic bottles, takeaway packaging, and souvenirs that never get used. The good news is that a lower-waste trip is not about sacrifice. It is about a little planning before you leave, a small kit of reusables, and choices that usually save money too. This guide shows how to plan an eco-friendly holiday that is lighter on your luggage, your wallet, and the places you visit.

Start With How You Get There

Transport is usually the biggest environmental cost of a holiday, so it deserves the first decision. For shorter trips, trains and coaches generally produce far less pollution per passenger than flying, and they often drop you right in the centre of town. If you do fly, choose a direct flight where possible, since take-off and landing use the most fuel, and pack light, because lighter planes burn less fuel. Once you arrive, walking, cycling, and public transport let you see far more than a hire car does, and many cities offer cheap day passes for buses and trams.

Pack a Reusable Travel Kit

A small reusable kit prevents most holiday waste before it happens. Pack a water bottle you can refill, a cloth tote bag for markets and shops, a set of reusable cutlery, and a coffee cup if you drink takeaway coffee. Refillable toiletry bottles filled from your full-size products at home replace the tiny single-use bottles that hotels hand out, and solid shampoo and soap bars skip plastic packaging entirely and never leak in your bag. A zero-waste travel kit built once can be reused for years, and most of the items are things you already own. Keep the kit packed between trips so it is ready to grab, and add a couple of cloth napkins and a small container for leftovers or market snacks.

Choose Where You Stay

Where you sleep shapes your trip's footprint. Smaller guesthouses and locally owned stays usually keep more of your money in the local economy than large international chains. Look for places that mention real practices, such as refillable soap dispensers, linen reuse programmes, and recycling, rather than vague claims about being green. A stay with a kitchenette lets you cook simple meals, shop at local markets, and cut packaging waste from eating out three times a day. If your accommodation offers a laundry service, use it once mid-trip instead of packing twice the clothes, which keeps your bag lighter and your wardrobe smaller.

Eat and Shop Like a Local

Food is one of the best parts of travel, and it is also one of the easiest places to cut waste. Eat where locals eat, choose restaurants that cook with seasonal ingredients, and try the market instead of the supermarket for snacks and picnic supplies. Carry your tote bag and containers, say no to plastic straws and cutlery you do not need, and order only what you will finish. When you buy souvenirs, choose things that are useful, locally made, and easy to carry home: a jar of local honey, a handmade cloth, or a small craft item beats a mass-produced keyring that ends up in a drawer. Reusable products that save money at home do the same job on holiday, and the savings add up across a whole trip.

Leave Places Better Than You Found Them

The simplest rule of eco-friendly travel is to leave no trace. Take all your rubbish with you on hikes and beach days, stay on marked paths in fragile landscapes, and never take shells, stones, or plants as souvenirs. Be careful with water in dry destinations: take short showers, reuse towels, and carry your refillable bottle instead of buying new ones. If you visit wildlife, choose responsible operators that keep a respectful distance and do not allow feeding or touching. Small courtesies like these protect the places that make travel worthwhile, and they cost nothing.

A Simple Lower-Waste Packing List

  • Refillable water bottle, cloth tote bag, and reusable cutlery set.
  • Refillable toiletry bottles or solid shampoo, conditioner, and soap bars.
  • Cloth napkins, a small food container, and a reusable coffee cup.
  • Versatile clothes you can wash and rewear, in one carry-on if possible.
  • A paper map or offline maps downloaded before you leave.
  • A small laundry bag and a bar of soap for hand-washing clothes.

The Bottom Line

Eco-friendly travel is mostly good travel: slower journeys, local food, lighter bags, and places left clean for the next visitor. Plan your transport, pack your reusable kit, and choose local wherever you can. The holiday will feel richer, not restricted, and the waste you do not create is the easiest waste of all to deal with.
